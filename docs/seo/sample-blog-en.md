## How to Choose a Perfume That Actually Lasts

Ever bought a perfume you loved in-store, only to have it fade an hour after you leave the house? You're not imagining it — how long a fragrance lasts depends on more than the bottle. Here's what actually matters, and how to shop for staying power.

### 1. Scent family changes everything

Lighter families — citrus, aquatic, green — are designed to be fresh and short-lived by nature. Warmer families — oud, amber, vanilla, musk — sit closer to the skin and build over hours. If longevity is your priority, start there.

### 2. Concentration matters more than the brand

Eau de toilette, eau de parfum, and parfum aren't marketing labels — they're different concentrations of fragrance oil. Higher concentration generally means it lasts longer and projects less loudly right away. Check the label, not just the name.

### 3. Your skin plays a role too

Dry skin doesn't hold fragrance as well as oily or well-moisturized skin. A quick fix: apply an unscented moisturizer to pulse points before your perfume — it gives the scent something to hold onto.

### 4. Where you spray it

Pulse points — wrists, neck, behind the ears — generate heat, which helps the fragrance diffuse and last. Spraying on clothing can actually make scent fade faster, since fabric doesn't hold fragrance oils the way skin does.
